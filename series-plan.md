# The Observatory explained: plan of the series

Audience: a general audience skewed to the technical, at the level of second-year computer science students. Form: a series of short episodes, first made as one long video of about 48 minutes and cut later. Visuals: animations and screenshots.

Each episode opens on the animated building plan with its room lit up and one sentence on what it explains. Animations keep the colours of flow.png: orange for a step that calls a model, blue for ordinary code, purple for a person, grey for something stored. Model names appear on screen only; the narration says "a cheap model" or "a stronger model", so the narration does not date. One real story, taken from the archive on observatory.mgorsky.net, runs through episodes 2 to 7.

## Episodes

1. A news agency with no staff (about 4 minutes). The problem, the two desks, the publications, the building plan, and documents before code.
2. The wire room: many sources, one shape (about 5 minutes). Collectors, the connector, fingerprints, fallbacks, the YouTube limit, the transcription queue. Ideas: adapters, normalisation, deduplication.
3. The barrier: cheap judgments first (about 5 minutes). The cascade, the second opinion, measured losses, the Pre-Book. Ideas: classifier cascades, recall before precision.
4. The newsroom: choosing and checking (about 6 minutes). Selection in one batch, the reserve, translation, the credibility check, length limits, tags, the final duplicate check. Ideas: batch against stream, retry with fallback, controlled vocabularies.
5. The Book: where every decision is kept (about 5 minutes). Two SQLite files, versioned settings, provenance of each note. Ideas: data lifetimes, reproducibility.
6. The print room: one issue, many outlets (about 6 minutes). Publications as specifications, watermarks, adapters, subscribers' hours and languages, the spoken daily and the terminal shorts.
7. The readers' room: answering questions (about 4 minutes). Retrieval before generation, access control, outbound-only connections, two clocks.
8. The machine room: running it all on one Mac (about 5 minutes). Five lanes, call priorities, one copy of the app, health checks, alerts, the live admin panel.
9. Choosing models and paying for them (about 5 minutes). Benchmarks with right answers from a stronger model, a person decides, spending limits, cost drift.
10. How it was built and what comes next (about 3 minutes). House rules, Claude Code and the commit record, unfinished work, the running story reaches a reader.
